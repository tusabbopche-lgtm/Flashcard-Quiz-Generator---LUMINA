Cells are the basic units of life. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells. 
Mitochondria are often referred to as the powerhouse of the cell because they generate most of the cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), used as a source of chemical energy. 
Mitochondria have their own DNA and are believed to have evolved from ancient bacteria through endosymbiosis.
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms use sunlight to synthesize foods with the help of chlorophyll. 
In plants, photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts, primarily in the leaves. The process converts carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen using light energy.
Cell division is essential for growth, repair, and reproduction. Mitosis produces two identical daughter cells, while meiosis produces four genetically different gametes.
The cell membrane controls the movement of substances in and out of the cell. It is selectively permeable, allowing some molecules to pass while blocking others.
Ribosomes are responsible for protein synthesis. They can be found free in the cytoplasm or attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.
The nucleus contains the cell's genetic material, DNA, which directs all cellular activities and is passed from one generation to the next.
Enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions without being consumed in the process. Each enzyme is specific to a particular substrate.
